# SOP-QA-014: Calibration of Laboratory Balances

Version 3.0 · Effective 2026-02-01 · Owner: Quality Assurance

## 1. Purpose

This procedure describes how analytical and precision balances are calibrated and verified so that weighing results used in testing and release remain accurate and traceable to national standards.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to all balances in the Quality Control and Production laboratories. It covers daily verification, scheduled external calibration and the handling of a balance that fails verification. It does not cover process scales on the packaging line, which are governed by SOP-ENG-022.

## 3. Definitions

- **Verification**: a quick check with certified check weights to confirm the balance still performs within limits.
- **Calibration**: a full adjustment and measurement of the balance against traceable standards, performed by an accredited provider.
- **Tolerance**: the maximum permitted deviation, set at 0.1 % of the check weight for analytical balances.

## 4. Responsibilities

The laboratory analyst performs the daily verification before first use and records the result. The laboratory supervisor reviews the log weekly and decides whether a balance may stay in service. Quality Assurance schedules external calibration, approves the calibration certificates and keeps the equipment register. The calibration provider supplies a certificate for every balance calibrated.

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Daily verification

1. Confirm the balance is level by checking the bubble indicator, and adjust the feet if needed.
2. Switch the balance on and let it warm up for at least 30 minutes.
3. Clean the weighing pan with a lint-free cloth. Never use solvents on the pan.
4. Place the certified check weight in the centre of the pan using forceps, never bare hands.
5. Record the displayed value in the balance log, with date, time, balance ID and analyst initials.
6. Compare the reading with the nominal weight. If the deviation is inside tolerance, the balance may be used. If it is outside tolerance, repeat once after cleaning the pan.
7. If the second reading is also outside tolerance, tag the balance "Out of Service" and notify the supervisor immediately.

### 5.2 Scheduled calibration

1. Quality Assurance generates the calibration schedule each January from the equipment register. Each balance is calibrated every 12 months.
2. Notify affected teams at least 10 working days before the calibration date and arrange temporary use of a backup balance.
3. The provider calibrates the balance on site and issues a certificate showing the measured deviations and the standards used.
4. Quality Assurance reviews the certificate within 5 working days. The balance is released only if every measured point is within tolerance.
5. Attach a new calibration label showing the date calibrated and the next due date.

### 5.3 Failed verification or calibration

1. Tag the balance "Out of Service" and remove it from the work area.
2. Review all results obtained with the balance since its last successful verification and assess the impact on released batches.
3. Open a deviation report in the quality system and follow SOP-QA-007 for investigation.
4. Return the balance to service only after repair, successful calibration and Quality Assurance approval.

## 6. Records

The balance log, calibration certificates and deviation reports are kept for at least 5 years after the balance is retired. Records are stored in the quality document archive and must be available for inspection.

## 7. References

SOP-QA-007 Deviation Management; SOP-ENG-022 Process Scale Control; ISO/IEC 17025 general requirements for the competence of testing and calibration laboratories.
